# Day 172 — Capstone Build 4: Observability + SLOs

Phase 6 · Capstone build sprint, day 4 of 7.

---

## Recall warm-up (5 min)
1. What did `argocd app diff` prove about the deployment yesterday?
2. Walk through the break-and-rollback drill from Day 171 in one sentence.
3. Why does CI stop at the manifest/tag update instead of deploying directly?

## Objectives (today)
1. Instrument the capstone service with metrics, logs, and (if time allows)
   traces, matching Phase 5's stack (Prometheus/Grafana/Loki/OTel).
2. Define at least two SLOs with real burn-rate alerts, per Day 121/130's pattern.

## Deliverables
- Metrics exported and scraped (USE/RED-method coverage, Day 124), visible on a
  Grafana dashboard dedicated to this service.
- Structured logs shipped to the existing Loki stack with useful labels
  (Day 127–128).
- Two SLOs defined in writing (e.g., availability, latency) with recording rules
  and multi-window burn-rate alerts (Day 130 pattern), routed through the existing
  Alertmanager config.
- A runbook entry for each new alert (Day 125 discipline — no alert without a
  runbook).

## Suggested time-box (3h)
- **0:00–0:20** — Recap USE (resource-focused) vs RED (request-focused) methods
  and multi-window burn-rate math (Day 130) before instrumenting.
- **0:20–1:20** — Add metrics instrumentation to the service code; verify scraping;
  build the Grafana dashboard.
- **1:20–1:50** — Ship logs to Loki with structured labels; confirm LogQL queries
  work against real traffic.
- **1:50–2:40** — Write the SLOs, recording rules, and burn-rate alerting rules;
  route through Alertmanager; write the two runbooks.
- **2:40–3:00** — Checkpoint review, using real traffic (generate some via a
  quick script or manual curls) to confirm dashboards and alerts reflect reality,
  not just static config.

## Checkpoint (trainer confirms before Day 173 starts)
- Dashboard shows live, moving data under real traffic — not a static/empty panel.
- At least one burn-rate alert has been intentionally triggered (via synthetic
  load or error injection) and correctly fired and routed.
- Both new alerts have committed runbooks.

## Trainer-review questions
1. What are your two SLOs, in plain English, and why those two for this service?
2. Show me the burn-rate alert firing — what traffic/error pattern triggered it?
3. What would an on-call engineer do first, per your runbook, if this alert fired
   at 3am?
4. What's missing from today's observability that you'd add with more time?

## Definition of done
Metrics + logs live for the capstone service, dashboard reflects real traffic, two
SLOs with working burn-rate alerts routed through Alertmanager, runbooks committed,
checkpoint confirmed with a real triggered-alert drill.
