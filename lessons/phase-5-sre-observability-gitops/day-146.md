# Day 146 — SRE Interview Prep: Drills, "Design Monitoring for X", SLO Math

Phase 5 · follows Day 145 (game day live incident).
Format: this is a **session-plan day** — the instructor facilitates timed
interview-style drills rather than teaching new tools.

---

## Recall warm-up (5 min, from Day 145)
1. What's the difference between time-to-mitigate and time-to-root-cause?
2. What made the game day postmortem's action items "good" rather than vague?
3. Name one thing the game day revealed as a gap in tooling or runbooks.

## Session plan (2h, timed)

### Block 1 — Troubleshooting drills (0:00–0:40, 4×10 min)
Instructor presents a symptom verbally (no terminal); the learner has 8 minutes to
narrate a full diagnostic path, then 2 minutes of feedback. Pull scenarios
from the phase's real material — don't invent contrived puzzles:
1. "p99 latency doubled at 14:00, error rate unchanged. Where do you look
   first, second, third?" (expects: dashboard → check if it's one
   endpoint or all → traces → recent deploys via ArgoCD history)
2. "ArgoCD shows `project-app` as `OutOfSync` and it won't self-heal.
   What are the possible causes?" (expects: automated sync disabled,
   sync failing due to a manifest error, RBAC issue, resource hook stuck)
3. "Alertmanager fired `ErrorBudgetBurnFast` but the RED dashboard looks
   fine. What do you check?" (expects: check the exact PromQL window,
   check if it already resolved, check for a metric/label mismatch —
   a debugging-the-monitoring-itself scenario)
4. "A canary at 20% traffic shows worse latency than the stable version.
   Walk through your decision to abort or continue." (expects: check
   sample size/statistical significance, check absolute vs relative
   impact, err toward abort given asymmetric downside)

### Block 2 — "Design monitoring for X" (0:40–1:20, 2×20 min)
Classic SRE system-design-adjacent interview question. The learner designs
out loud (whiteboard or paper), instructor probes:
1. "Design monitoring and alerting for a payment processing API." (expects:
   SLIs on success rate + latency, distinguish client errors (4xx) from
   server errors (5xx), idempotency-aware retry monitoring, burn-rate
   alerts given money is at stake, PCI-adjacent log redaction awareness)
2. "Design monitoring for a batch job that runs nightly." (expects:
   different SLI shape — completion + duration + freshness of output, not
   request-rate based; alert on "didn't run" and "ran but produced stale/
   wrong data", not just crash)
- Probe questions to inject: "what's your SLI here, precisely?", "how do
  you avoid paging on a false positive?", "what's the runbook for this alert?"

### Block 3 — SLO math under pressure (1:20–1:50, 30 min)
Rapid-fire, no notes, answer out loud within 60 seconds each:
1. 99.9% SLO over 30 days — how many minutes of error budget?
2. Current burn rate is 6x — roughly how long until the budget is gone,
   given a 30-day window?
3. Error budget is 60% consumed with 10 days left in the window — should
   you ship a risky feature today? Why or why not?
4. Convert a 99.95% availability target into expected downtime per year.
5. Two services each at 99.9% availability, called in series for one user
   request — what's the compound availability, roughly?

### Block 4 — Debrief (1:50–2:00)
- Identify the weakest of the three blocks; that becomes tonight's NotebookLM
  focus and a candidate topic to revisit before Day 149's mock interviews.

## Evaluation rubric
| Dimension | Weak | Solid | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Troubleshooting | Jumps to conclusions, skips dashboard/logs order | Follows a logical diagnostic sequence | Sequence + explains *why* each step, considers red herrings |
| System design | Lists tools with no SLI framing | Defines SLIs before naming tools | SLIs first, tradeoffs named, alert-fatigue avoided by design |
| SLO math | Wrong or no answer within time | Correct with a pause | Correct, fast, explains the "why" of the number |

**Definition of done:** all four troubleshooting drills attempted with
narrated reasoning; both design questions answered with SLIs stated
explicitly before tools; SLO math block completed within time; weakest
area identified for tonight's review.

## Recall questions
1. Why should SLIs be defined before naming monitoring tools in a design answer?
2. Why is a batch job's ideal SLI shape different from a request-driven API's?
3. If burn rate is 6x on a 30-day-window SLO, roughly how many days until
   the budget is exhausted?
4. Compound availability of two 99.9% services in series — is it higher or
   lower than 99.9%, and why?
