## Theory (1h)

### A. Cluster-autoscaler vs Karpenter concepts (20 min)
- **cluster-autoscaler**: watches for unschedulable pods (pending due to
  insufficient node capacity) and scales node groups/ASGs up; scales down
  underutilized nodes after a cooldown — works at the node-group level,
  reactive to pod scheduling pressure.
- **Karpenter**: a newer, more direct provisioner — watches pending pods
  and provisions right-sized nodes on demand (bin-packing aware, can mix
  instance types/Spot) without needing pre-defined node groups — faster
  scale-up, tighter bin-packing, hence lower cost per unit of capacity.
- On kind (local), neither runs for real — the concept is taught via
  reading manifests/docs and reasoning about the AWS-prod equivalent
  (EKS + Karpenter is the modern default recommendation over
  cluster-autoscaler for new setups).

### B. Pod Disruption Budgets — protecting availability during voluntary
disruption (15 min)
- A PDB (`minAvailable` or `maxUnavailable`) limits how many pods of a
  set can be down *at once* during voluntary disruptions (node drains,
  cluster-autoscaler scale-down, rolling updates) — it does not protect
  against involuntary disruption (a node crashing outright).
- Without a PDB, an autoscaler scale-down or `kubectl drain` can legally
  take out every replica of a Deployment simultaneously if they're all on
  the node being drained — the exact SLO-breaching failure mode this guards.

### C. Cost vs reliability — the FinOps tie-back (15 min)
- More replicas / more headroom (Day 138) = better latency tail and more
  chaos/scale-down tolerance, but higher steady-state cost — the Day
  117–118 FinOps lens applies directly here: rightsizing requests/limits,
  Spot for stateless/chaos-tolerant workloads, and reserved capacity for
  the baseline floor.
- The PDB is a *reliability* control that has a *cost* shadow: a strict
  `minAvailable` can block node drains/autoscaling entirely if replica
  count is too low relative to the budget — size both together.

### D. Bringing it together for the project (10 min)
- Today's decisions (autoscaling posture, PDB, replica count) should be
  justified against three anchors from this phase: the SLO/error budget
  (Day 121), the saturation point (Day 138), and cost (Phase 4's FinOps
  work) — not picked arbitrarily.

## Lab (2h)

### Part 1 — Read and reason about autoscaler configs (30 min)
1. Read the cluster-autoscaler and Karpenter docs/sample manifests (no
   cloud spend required); write a comparison table in
   `/opt/fresher_project/docs/autoscaling-notes.md`: trigger mechanism,
   granularity, typical scale-up latency, instance-type flexibility.
2. Decide and document which one you'd recommend for the project's
   eventual EKS home and why (tie to Day 104's EKS work and Day 118's
   FinOps rightsizing).

### Part 2 — Configure and test a PDB (40 min)
3. Write a `PodDisruptionBudget` for `project-app`: `minAvailable: 1` (or
   appropriate given current replica count).
4. Simulate a drain: `kubectl drain <node> --ignore-daemonsets
   --delete-emptydir-data` on the kind node hosting a `project-app` pod
   (if single-node kind, use `--dry-run=client` to reason through it, or
   scale kind to 2+ worker nodes for a real test).
5. Confirm the drain respects the PDB — it won't evict below `minAvailable`
   and will wait/fail rather than breach it.

### Part 3 — Re-run Day 138's stress test with the PDB and HPA active (35 min)
6. Ensure the Day 69 HPA is active on `project-app`; re-run the Day 138
   `stress.js` and watch replica count scale via `kubectl get hpa -w`.
7. While under stress, attempt a node drain again — confirm the PDB still
   holds even as HPA is trying to add replicas (a realistic
   "maintenance during traffic spike" scenario).

### Part 4 — Project step (15 min)
8. Compute a simple cost/reliability tradeoff note: current replica count
   × estimated per-pod cost vs headroom-to-saturation ratio from Day 138 —
   is the project over- or under-provisioned relative to expected traffic?
9. Commit the PDB manifest and both docs (`autoscaling-notes.md`,
   updated `capacity.md` with the tradeoff note).

**Definition of done:** PDB applied and verified against a drain attempt;
autoscaler comparison documented with an EKS recommendation; cost/
reliability tradeoff computed and recorded.
