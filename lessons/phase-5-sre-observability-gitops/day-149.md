## Session plan (2h, timed — mirrors a real interview loop's structure)

### Block 1 — Behavioral/resume-walkthrough round (0:00–0:25)
- Instructor plays interviewer, cold, using only the resume/README as context
  (no coaching mid-round). Ask: "Walk me through this project," "Tell me
  about a time something broke and how you handled it" (should draw on
  the real Day 145 game day), "Why does the project use Loki instead of
  the ELK stack?" (tests whether tradeoff reasoning, not just tool
  recall, is retained from Day 127).
- No feedback during the round — hold it for Block 4.

### Block 2 — Technical deep-dive round (0:25–0:55)
- Pick two areas at random from this phase and ask "senior engineer"
  depth questions, pushing past docs-level answers into failure modes:
  1. "Walk me through what happens end-to-end when you `git push` a new
     image tag, from CI to it actually serving traffic." (tests CI →
     registry → ArgoCD/image-updater → sync → rollout, Day 132/134)
  2. "Your canary at 20% shows a latency regression. What's your exact
     decision process?" (tests Day 136's judgment, not just the mechanic)
  3. "A chaos pod-kill experiment breached the SLO unexpectedly. What do
     you check first?" (tests Day 137's hypothesis-driven debugging, not
     panic-driven poking)
- Push on any hand-wavy answer: "why", "what if that's not true", "what
  would break this approach at 10x scale."

### Block 3 — Live troubleshooting round (0:55–1:35)
- Instructor injects a real, undisclosed fault into the project (same style as
  Day 145, smaller scope — pick one from the Day 137/144 toolbox) and
  gives a time-boxed 30 minutes to diagnose and propose a fix, narrating
  the whole time, exactly as a real pairing/screen-share interview round works.
- This round is scored on **process** (did they use dashboard → logs →
  traces → hypothesis → test in order) more than on reaching the exact
  root cause within the time box.

### Block 4 — Debrief and gap list (1:35–2:00)
- Instructor gives direct, specific feedback per round using the Day 111/121
  interaction-guide norms (be direct, reduce load, don't sugarcoat).
- Produce a written **gap list**: 3–5 specific weak spots (e.g. "hesitates
  on compound-availability math", "explains tools before explaining
  tradeoffs", "narrates diagnosis out of order under time pressure").
- Assign each gap a concrete fix-by-Day-150 or fix-in-Phase-6 action —
  this list is the direct input to what gets emphasized before Day 150's
  capstone write-up and Day 178's job sprint later in Phase 6.

## Evaluation rubric
| Dimension | Weak | Solid | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resume walkthrough | Recites tools | Explains what/why | Explains what/why + a real anecdote (game day) with outcome |
| Technical depth | Docs-level answers | Explains tradeoffs | Explains tradeoffs + failure modes + scale implications |
| Live troubleshooting | No clear process, guesses | Follows dashboard→logs→traces order | Follows order + explains reasoning + adapts to a red herring |
| Receiving feedback | Defensive or dismissive | Accepts and restates | Accepts, restates, and proposes its own fix |

**Definition of done:** all three interview rounds completed; direct
feedback given per round; a written gap list of 3–5 items produced with
owners/timing.
