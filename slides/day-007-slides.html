How today works

Three rules

  • Answer before you look. "I don't know" is a useful answer — a dressed-up guess is the only wrong move
  • Being wrong is fine. I'm not scoring you, I'm mapping you. A miss today is free
  • Anything you fumble, we prove on the box. What you type sticks; what you hear doesn't
  • Why 1-3-7-21: memory falls off a cliff early, then flattens. Re-test just before the drop and the curve resets flatter each time. Four small efforts beat one cram.
Quiz · part 1

Boot, journal, redirection, accounts

  1. Order the boot stages: firmware, GRUB, kernel init, initramfs, PID 1 exec
  2. journalctl -b -1 — what does it show, and what must be true for it to work?
  3. > vs >> vs 2>&1 — and why does order matter between the two forms?
  4. What's in /etc/shadow that isn't in /etc/passwd, and why is it root-only?
  5. Why must usermod -G be paired with -a?
ls -d /var/log/journal  ·  ls /etc /nosuchdir 2>&1 > wrong.txt
Quiz · part 2

Permissions and text processing

  1. Convert rwxr-x--- to octal · what does SGID do on a directory?
  2. How does an ACL grant differ from standard rwx?
  3. BRE vs ERE — which metacharacters need -E to work unescaped?
  4. Why does uniq -c need pre-sorted input · what's the "most common first" idiom?
  5. In awk: NF, NR, $0 — and how do you set a custom field separator?
getfacl /opt/fresher_project  ·  awk -F: '{print $7}' /etc/passwd | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn
Block 2 · ~45 min, yours

Redo your weakest lab

  • Pick the one you were least confident in — usually Day 4 ACLs, Day 5 quantifiers, or Day 6 awk fields
  • Redo it from scratch with the day file CLOSED. Work from the goal, reconstruct the commands
  • man is allowed. The day file is not. Get stuck, sit in it, try something
  • Then compare — and write the gap down specifically. "I guessed 4000 for SGID, it's 2000." That list is worth more than the redo.
